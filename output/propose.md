We are standing at a precipice. Large Language Models have moved from experimental curiosities to silent architects of our information ecosystem. They draft our emails, guide our diagnoses, write our news, and tutor our children. Yet this unprecedented concentration of influence operates within a legal vacuum, governed only by the voluntary ethical guidelines of private corporations. That is unacceptable. Strict laws are not an impediment to innovation; they are the necessary scaffolding for a technology that can either elevate or dismantle our society.

First, consider the scale of harm. LLMs are not neutral tools. They hallucinate with confidence, generate deepfake disinformation at near-zero cost, and can be weaponized to perpetrate fraud, manipulate elections, or automate identity theft. Current laws treat the *user* as the sole responsible party. But the user is often a victim themselves—misled by a model that presents falsehoods with the authority of a trusted oracle. Without strict laws imposing liability on the developers and deployers, there is no incentive to build safe systems. We do not let pharmaceutical companies sell untested drugs and then blame the patient for taking them. Why should we let AI companies deploy unverifiable models that can poison public discourse?

Second, the principle of accountability is broken. When an LLM defames a person, denies a loan, or provides dangerous medical advice, who is sued? The algorithm has no wallet. The company hides behind terms-of-service and “beta” disclaimers. Strict regulation would mandate clear chains of responsibility: every output should be traceable, every high-stakes deployment should require licensing, and every harm should have a legal remedy. This is not censorship; it is the bedrock of a functional legal system.

Third, the asymmetry of power demands legal intervention. A single LLM has been trained on a trillion words and is deployed to billions of people. The average citizen cannot audit its biases, cannot inspect its training data, and cannot refuse its influence in their daily life—from automated customer service to government portals. Strict laws are the only mechanism that can force transparency: mandated disclosure of training sources, mandatory red-team testing for dangerous capabilities, and independent third-party audits. Without these, we are ceding our public square to an unaccountable black box.

The counter-argument—that strict laws will stifle innovation—is a false dichotomy. History shows that transformative technologies flourish within clear legal guardrails. The automobile required traffic laws. Aviation required safety standards. Banks required capital requirements. In each case, regulation did not kill the industry; it built public trust and enabled mass adoption. LLMs are no different. A strict legal framework would actually catalyze innovation by defining a clear market for safe, compliant AI—rewarding companies that prioritize correctness and ethics, rather than those that simply launch the fastest.

Finally, there is the existential dimension. LLMs are accelerating. They now write code that runs our infrastructure. They are beginning to negotiate, persuade, and act autonomously. If we wait for a catastrophe—a market crash triggered by AI-generated lies, a deadly medical misdiagnosis, an election subverted—to write laws in panic, we will be too late. Strict regulation must be proactive, setting hard limits on deployment in critical sectors, requiring human oversight for consequential decisions, and imposing criminal penalties for gross negligence.

To do nothing is a decision. It is a decision to let private profit dictate the limits of truth, safety, and accountability. The motion before us is not about restricting technology; it is about protecting human agency. We need strict laws because the alternative is an unregulated wild west where the most powerful tool ever invented operates with zero checks and balances. That is not progress. That is recklessness enshrined in silicon.

Vote in favor of strict regulation. Vote for accountability. Vote for the right of every citizen to know that the words they read, the advice they follow, and the decisions they make are not the outputs of an ungoverned machine. Thank you.
